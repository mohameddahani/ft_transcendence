# Gym Member Retention: The Complete Guide (PushPress)

*A summary in our own words of [the original article](https://www.pushpress.com/blog/gym-member-retention-guide). Numbers in it appear in the original.*

## Key points

*   **Retention is Key to Growth:** Many gyms mistakenly believe they need more new members, but the real issue is often keeping existing ones. If your membership numbers stay the same despite new sign-ups, you're not growing; you're just replacing lost members.
*   **Financial Impact of Retention:** Improving member retention significantly boosts your gym's profitability and predictability. A small reduction in members leaving can lead to a substantial increase in the total revenue each member generates over their time with you.
*   **Retention Lifecycle:** Member retention is a continuous process with five stages: Awareness, Recruitment, Engagement, Renewal, and Reinstatement. The biggest opportunities for improvement lie in the Engagement, Renewal, and Reinstatement phases.
*   **Retention Outperforms Acquisition:** It is considerably more expensive to attract a new member than to retain an existing one. Even small improvements in retention rates can lead to significant financial gains, making your business more stable and less reliant on constant marketing efforts.
*   **Calculating Retention:** To improve, you must measure. You can calculate your monthly churn rate (members lost divided by members at the start, multiplied by one hundred) and your monthly retention rate (one hundred percent minus your churn rate).
*   **Industry Benchmarks:** A monthly churn rate below five percent is considered good, three to four percent is strong, and under three percent is elite. An annual retention rate of seventy-five to eighty percent is a good target for boutique studios, while anything below sixty-six percent suggests fundamental issues, often in the first three months.
*   **Five Mindsets for High Retention:**
    *   **Friendliness:** Make your gym the most welcoming place. Implement rules like greeting members within three feet and ensuring every member receives three instances of their name used, eye contact, and appropriate physical contact during a session.
    *   **Cleanliness:** Maintain a spotless environment. This includes organized belongings, a clear equipment put-away system, clean bathrooms, and a pleasant scent.
    *   **Class Quality:** Strive for excellence in every class, as members can lose interest quickly. Implement peer evaluations for coaches and encourage end-of-class commitments from members to return.
    *   **Constructive Feedback:** Provide members with both intrinsic (self-discovered) and extrinsic (coach-given) feedback. Use a "Praise-Correct-Praise" approach, focusing on what members do well before offering corrections.
    *   **Referrals:** Recognize that your best new members often come from existing ones. Ask current members for referrals sincerely and acknowledge every referral they provide.
*   **The First Ninety Days are Crucial:** Most members who leave do so within the first three months. Focus on making this period intentional by:
    *   **Following Up on Missed Classes:** Send a personal text within minutes of a new member missing a class.
    *   **Identifying What They Love:** Ask new members what they enjoy most about the gym to reinforce their commitment.
    *   **Painting a Picture of Success:** Help members visualize their long-term progress through check-ins, examples of other members, and tracking their benchmarks.
*   **Consistency is Key:** Members who check in twelve or more times a month have a significantly lower chance of leaving compared to those who check in only once.

## What a gym can do

*   **Implement Missed-Class Texts:** Train coaches to send a personal text within five minutes of a new member missing a class, asking if they can make it up.
*   **Encourage End-of-Class Commitments:** Have coaches ask every member at the end of class if they will be back for the next session.
*   **Apply the 3x3 Rule:** Ensure staff and tenured members greet people within three feet of the door and that every member receives three instances of their name used, eye contact, and appropriate physical contact during each session.
*   **Adopt the Praise-Correct-Praise Feedback Loop:** Train coaches to provide feedback using this method, starting new instructors by focusing on spotting what members do well.
*   **Assign New Member Owners:** Designate a specific coach to be responsible for each new member's first thirty days, including asking what they love about the gym and following up on missed classes.
*   **Systematize Referral Requests:** Encourage sincere, one-on-one referral asks from coaches, track who has been asked, and acknowledge every referral with a thank you.
*   **Conduct Weekly Class-Quality Audits:** Have coaches evaluate each other's classes weekly using a simple rubric to maintain high standards.
*   **Utilize Software for Retention:** Leverage gym management software to identify at-risk members, celebrate attendance milestones, track member notes and history, automate welcome series and milestone celebrations, and gather feedback to catch problems early.
*   **Focus on the First Ninety Days:** Develop a structured plan for new members that includes consistent follow-ups, goal setting, and celebrating small victories to build habits and connections.
*   **Part on Good Terms:** When a member cancels, treat it as a conversation to understand their reasons, and if they still leave, ensure it's on good terms for potential future win-back campaigns.
