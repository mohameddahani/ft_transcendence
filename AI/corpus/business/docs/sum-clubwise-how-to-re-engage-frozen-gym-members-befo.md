# How to Re-engage Frozen Gym Members Before They Cancel (ClubWise)

*A summary in our own words of [the original article](https://www.clubwise.com/clubwise-blog/re-engaging-the-sleeper-fixing-frozen-gym-memberships/). Numbers in it appear in the original.*

## Key points

*   **Frozen members are a serious threat:** Members who are still paying but not attending are likely to cancel soon. They often drift away quietly, and by the time you notice, it's hard to win them back.
*   **The problem compounds over time:** Inactivity for a month leads to guilt, which fades by the second month, and by the third month, they've mentally cancelled. This often results in sudden cancellations that hurt your cash flow.
*   **Financial impact is significant:** Beyond lost membership fees, you lose the initial investment made to acquire that member. Long-term members are much more valuable, and early cancellations reduce your overall revenue.
*   **Retention is cheaper than acquisition:** It costs much more to get a lapsed member back than to keep an existing one. If they join a competitor, you might never see them again.
*   **Reasons for inactivity are often personal:** Members usually go cold due to life events like new jobs, family illness, holidays, injuries, or shifting priorities, not because they're unhappy with your gym. Their routine is broken, but the desire for fitness often remains.
*   **Psychological barriers exist:** Especially for newer members, returning after a break can feel intimidating due to worries about judgment or not knowing where to start.
*   **Intervention window is critical:** Habits form or break in about two to four weeks. If a member misses two consecutive weeks, they are at a high risk of becoming effectively churned. This is the best time to intervene.
*   **Personalised communication is key:** Automated messages should feel personal, using the member's name and referencing their specific situation, rather than generic greetings.
*   **Offers can help:** For members who have been inactive for a longer period, incentives like a complimentary personal training session, an invitation to an event, or a membership freeze option can encourage their return.
*   **Different channels work for different situations:** SMS is good for urgent messages, email for more detailed communication, and a personal call from staff for high-risk members.
*   **Cancellations are learning opportunities:** Even if a member cancels, a structured exit conversation can reveal reasons for leaving and help you prevent similar issues with other members. It's also a chance to offer alternatives like a lower-tier membership or a freeze.
*   **Lapsed members can be won back:** Former members are more likely to rejoin their old gym than a new one, especially if they had a positive experience. Win-back campaigns can recover lost revenue.
*   **Significant revenue impact:** Gyms typically lose between twenty and forty percent of members annually. Re-engaging even a small percentage of at-risk members can lead to substantial additional annual revenue.

## What a gym can do

*   **Implement automated tracking:** Use gym management software to monitor attendance patterns for all members and automatically flag those who show signs of inactivity.
*   **Set tiered absence triggers:** Configure your system to identify members at different risk levels based on their absence duration (e.g., ten days, twenty-one days, thirty days).
*   **Send timely, light-touch communications:** For members showing early signs of inactivity (e.g., ten days), send a friendly, non-heavy check-in message.
*   **Escalate communication for higher risk:** For members inactive for longer periods (e.g., twenty-one days), send a more personal message, possibly including an offer or incentive to encourage their return.
*   **Prioritise personal outreach for highest risk:** For members at serious risk of cancelling (e.g., thirty days), have a staff member make a personal phone call or send a genuinely personal email.
*   **Personalise messages:** Ensure all re-engagement messages use the member's name and reference their specific history, like a class they attended or a goal they were working towards.
*   **Offer incentives to return:** Provide options like a free personal training session, an invitation to a special event, or a membership freeze to make returning easier.
*   **Utilise multiple communication channels:** Send messages via SMS for immediacy, email for more detail, and personal calls for high-risk members.
*   **Conduct exit conversations:** When a member cancels, use a survey, phone call, or automated email to understand their reasons and potentially offer alternatives like a different membership tier or a freeze.
*   **Implement win-back campaigns:** Reach out to lapsed members at regular intervals (e.g., thirty, sixty, and ninety days after cancellation) with relevant offers to encourage them to rejoin.
