# Fitness Industry Statistics 2026: Membership, Revenue, and Retention Data (ABC Fitness)

*A summary in our own words of [the original article](https://abcfitness.com/abc-articles/fitness-industry-statistics/). Numbers in it appear in the original.*

## Key points

*   The global fitness industry is experiencing significant growth, with its value projected to nearly double by 2032. North America accounts for a substantial portion of this market.
*   Gym memberships are at record highs in both the USA and Europe, with a significant percentage of the population holding memberships.
*   The industry shows strong financial health, with median revenue growth and positive profit margins for most operators.
*   However, member retention is a challenge, with roughly one in three members leaving annually. Cancellations for traditional gyms increased, while studio cancellations decreased.
*   Personal training is a major revenue driver, contributing almost half of global health club revenue, and is expected to continue growing.
*   Group training members tend to stay longer than those who only use the gym floor.
*   Technology adoption is high, especially among younger generations, with many using AI-powered fitness apps and wearables.
*   The fitness industry supports a large number of jobs, and trainer roles are projected to grow, but attracting and retaining talent remains a key challenge.
*   The market is increasingly splitting into high-volume, low-price models and premium, high-touch models, putting pressure on clubs in the middle.
*   A significant portion of cancellations are due to failed payments, often linked to the rise of new banking methods among younger members.
*   Most member cancellations occur within the first few months, highlighting the importance of early engagement.
*   Regular visits and staff interactions significantly improve member retention.
*   The decline in average personal training session frequency suggests a shift towards hybrid coaching models.

## What a gym can do

*   **Focus on retention:** Since a third of members leave annually, even small improvements in retention can significantly impact your bottom line.
*   **Prioritize early engagement:** The first few months are critical. Implement strong onboarding programs to help new members establish a routine and connect with your facility.
*   **Promote group activities:** Encourage members to join group training or community-led formats, as these members tend to stay longer.
*   **Integrate personal training:** Make personal training visible, accessible, and easy to book from the start of a member's journey, rather than treating it as a separate service. Consider offering hybrid coaching options to cater to changing preferences.
*   **Leverage technology:** Embrace mobile apps for member self-service (joining, booking, payments, etc.). Integrate with wearables and AI-powered tools to meet member expectations for tracking, feedback, and personalization.
*   **Address payment issues:** Implement smart billing and payment protection strategies to reduce cancellations caused by failed payments, which are often fixable infrastructure problems.
*   **Empower your staff:** Recognize that your floor team is a crucial retention tool. Encourage staff interactions with members during visits, as even one interaction can significantly increase the likelihood of a member returning.
*   **Understand your market position:** Decide if you will operate as a high-volume, low-price gym or a premium, high-touch facility, as clubs in the middle are facing the most pressure.
*   **Design for diverse demographics:** Cater to both younger members (Gen Z), who join frequently but also churn quickly and expect digital and community aspects, and older members (65+), who have longer tenures and lower churn rates.
*   **Invest in your workforce:** With projected growth in trainer roles and staffing challenges, focus on attracting and retaining top talent. Consider using technology to enhance your existing team's capabilities rather than just adding headcount.
