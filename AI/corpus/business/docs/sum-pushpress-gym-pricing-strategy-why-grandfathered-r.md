# Gym Pricing Strategy: Why Grandfathered Rates Quietly Kill Profitable Gyms (PushPress)

*A summary in our own words of [the original article](https://www.pushpress.com/blog/gym-pricing-strategy-grandfathered-rates). Numbers in it appear in the original.*

## Key points

*   **Grandfathered rates are problematic:** Offering "locked-in-for-life" membership prices, often called founder rates, is a common and costly mistake. While they help attract initial members, they create a long-term financial burden.
*   **Costs increase, revenue doesn't:** Your gym's operating costs, such as rent, equipment, and staff salaries, will inevitably rise over time. Grandfathered rates mean a portion of your members continue to pay a price based on your initial, lower costs, which can severely impact your profit margins.
*   **Limits future investment:** When a significant number of members are paying outdated rates, it reduces your ability to invest in gym improvements like new equipment, coach training, or additional services. This lack of investment can hinder your gym's growth and quality.
*   **Creates a two-tier system:** Grandfathered rates can lead to resentment among members. Those paying current rates may feel unfairly treated compared to "OG" members who boast about their lower prices, making future price adjustments more difficult.
*   **Impacts your bottom line:** Frozen rates, combined with rising costs, force you to absorb the difference. This can lead to reduced owner pay, underpaid staff, declining class quality, and a scarcity mindset that postpones necessary purchases and marketing efforts.
*   **Signals a focus on price over value:** Offering permanent discounts suggests you're competing on price rather than the value and quality of your services. This can damage your brand perception and attract members who are primarily price-sensitive.

## What a gym can do

*   **Implement annual price increases:** The most effective solution is to build a small, predictable annual price increase, typically between three and five percent, directly into your membership strategy.
*   **Communicate clearly upfront:** Inform new members about the annual price increase as part of their membership agreement. This makes it an expected part of the deal, reducing surprises and difficult conversations later.
*   **Justify increases with value:** Use the annual increase as motivation to continuously improve your gym. New programs, better coaching, facility upgrades, or new amenities can justify the price adjustment to members.
*   **Address existing grandfathered rates:** If you already have members on grandfathered rates, be honest with them about the need for an increase due to rising costs and gym improvements.
*   **Tie increases to visible value:** When adjusting legacy rates, highlight the new equipment, programs, or certifications that demonstrate the increased value of the gym.
*   **Phase in increases:** Instead of a single large jump, consider phasing in the price increase for legacy members over two or three steps to make it more manageable for them.
*   **Be prepared for some attrition:** Understand that some members who were only staying for the discount might leave. This is not a failure, as these members were not contributing to your gym's long-term profitability.
*   **Integrate new pricing into all future agreements:** Once legacy rates are adjusted, ensure all new membership agreements include the annual price increase policy to avoid repeating the same mistake.
