# Gym Member Retention Strategies to Stop New Member Drop-Off (Glofox)

*A summary in our own words of [the original article](https://www.glofox.com/blog/gym-member-retention-strategies/). Numbers in it appear in the original.*

## Key points

*   The fitness industry's annual member retention rate is around two-thirds, meaning about one in three members leave each year. This drop-off isn't limited to the initial three months.
*   Focusing on member engagement can lead to a six percent reduction in cancellations year-over-year.
*   Members who stay longer, for example, eighteen months, are more likely to book personal training, buy retail items, and refer new members, unlike those who cancel early.
*   Members often leave not because they lose interest in fitness, but because they feel lost, unnoticed, or unsure if they made the right choice.
*   Early signs of disengagement include a drop in visit frequency, not meeting engagement milestones in the first few months, and infrequent staff interactions.
*   Clunky billing processes can also cause cancellations unrelated to a member's motivation.
*   High-retention gyms view retention as an ongoing process throughout a member's entire journey, not just a one-time onboarding effort.
*   Members who regularly visit for three months tend to integrate fitness into their routine, while those who skip weeks are more likely to consider canceling.
*   Community is a significant factor, with over half of active consumers stating social connection as a primary reason for joining a fitness community.
*   Members who only use the gym are more than fifty percent more likely to cancel than those who participate in group classes.
*   Technology is crucial for tracking member behavior and intervening before disengagement leads to cancellation.

## What a gym can do

*   Implement structured onboarding that goes beyond a facility tour, including a welcome email within twenty-four hours, a goal discussion and tour in the first week, and a progress review at day thirty. Consistent onboarding across all locations is important.
*   Provide clear guidance between visits, such as workout plans and class recommendations, to reduce friction and encourage consistent attendance. A branded member app can facilitate this.
*   Reinforce progress with early check-ins and celebrate small achievements in the first one to two months, like a member's fifth visit or first class booked. Automated milestone messages can help with this.
*   Utilize personalized communication at scale by segmenting members based on their behavior and preferences, then automating targeted messages for each group. This allows for relevant communication without needing more staff.
*   Offer habit-focused programming, such as multi-week challenges or progressive class series, instead of just open-ended access, to help members build routines.
*   Extend the member relationship beyond the physical gym with hybrid memberships and digital experiences, including on-demand workouts and a comprehensive mobile app for scheduling and communication.
*   Leverage AI-powered member engagement tools that can predict churn risk in advance, trigger automated outreach when usage dips, and assist with scheduling and staffing.
