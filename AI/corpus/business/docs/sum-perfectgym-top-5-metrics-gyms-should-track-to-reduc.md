# Top 5 Metrics Gyms Should Track to Reduce Member Churn (PerfectGym)

*A summary in our own words of [the original article](https://www.perfectgym.com/en/blog/business/key-metrics-gyms-should-track-to-reduce-member-churn). Numbers in it appear in the original.*

## Key points

*   **Churn is a big problem:** Losing members means less money and constant effort to find new ones. The average gym loses between 30% and 40% of its members each year, and some lose as many as 50%. Even a small improvement in keeping members can greatly increase your long-term profits.
*   **Engagement is key:** Members who use the gym regularly are much less likely to leave. Those who visit at least twice a week are 50% less likely to cancel than those who visit once a week or less.
*   **Early attendance matters:** Half of all new gym members stop coming within six months. This early period is crucial for keeping them.
*   **Class availability is important:** A report found that 81% of gym-goers say that being able to attend their favorite classes affects whether they stay.
*   **Happy members stay:** Gyms with a high Net Promoter Score (NPS) – above 50 – have retention rates that are 20% to 30% higher.
*   **Membership length:** The typical gym membership lasts about seven to eight months. Gyms that focus on keeping members can extend this to twelve months or more.
*   **Retention boosts profit:** Increasing member retention by just 5% can increase profits by 25% to 95%.

## What a gym can do

*   **Track member engagement:**
    *   Monitor how often members check in daily, weekly, and monthly.
    *   See who attends group classes and personal training sessions.
    *   Check how often members use your gym's app for bookings or tracking workouts.
    *   Observe participation in community events or social media groups.
    *   Reward frequent visits or consistent participation with loyalty programs.
    *   Offer personalized workout plans based on data.
    *   Send automated, personalized messages to inactive members to encourage them to return.
    *   Use challenges, leaderboards, and friendly competition to motivate members.
    *   Analyze engagement data regularly to adjust your offerings.
*   **Monitor attendance trends:**
    *   Compare attendance over 30, 60, and 90 days to spot members whose visits are decreasing.
    *   Identify and group members who are at risk of leaving based on declining attendance.
    *   Use data to predict which attendance patterns lead to cancellations.
    *   Track when members attend (peak vs. off-peak) to tailor re-engagement and optimize schedules.
    *   Send targeted messages to members showing declining attendance, offering incentives.
    *   Provide flexible options like virtual or on-demand classes.
    *   Encourage members to find workout buddies.
    *   Integrate with fitness tracking apps to help members monitor their progress.
*   **Optimize class and equipment use:**
    *   Track peak and off-peak usage for equipment and classes to improve scheduling.
    *   Monitor booking patterns and no-show rates for classes.
    *   Collect feedback from members about congestion or availability issues.
    *   Keep track of equipment downtime and maintenance schedules.
    *   Adjust class schedules based on demand.
    *   Implement a waitlist system for overbooked classes.
    *   Offer real-time availability tracking through a mobile app.
    *   Allocate equipment based on usage patterns, adding more popular machines if needed.
*   **Measure member satisfaction:**
    *   Send out Net Promoter Score (NPS) surveys quarterly to gauge how likely members are to recommend your gym.
    *   Always ask for feedback when members cancel their membership.
    *   Monitor online reviews and social media for real-time feedback.
    *   Analyze support tickets and complaints to identify recurring issues.
    *   Use feedback to personalize the gym experience.
    *   Communicate any improvements made based on member feedback.
    *   Consider upgrading your gym management system if it's causing member frustration.
*   **Understand membership length:**
    *   Track the average time members stay before canceling.
    *   Identify common points when members tend to cancel (e.g., after three or six months).
    *   Group members by their membership length and cancellation history to target retention efforts.
    *   Offer incentives to members nearing key cancellation points, like renewal discounts.
    *   Implement structured onboarding programs for new members.
    *   Reward members for staying longer with milestone perks.
    *   Offer tiered memberships with extra benefits for longer commitments.
