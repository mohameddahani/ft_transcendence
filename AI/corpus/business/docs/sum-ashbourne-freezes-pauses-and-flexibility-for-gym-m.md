# Freezes, Pauses and Flexibility for Gym Memberships (Ashbourne)

*A summary in our own words of [the original article](https://ashbourne-memberships.com/blog/2026/05/26/freezes-pauses-and-flexibility-what-actually-works/). Numbers in it appear in the original.*

## Key points

*   **Cancellations aren't always about service quality:** Members often cancel due to life events like injury, holidays, extra work, or financial difficulties, not because they're unhappy with your gym.
*   **Flexibility boosts retention:** Offering options like membership freezes or pauses can turn a potential cancellation into a reason for a member to stay, strengthening their bond with your independent gym.
*   **The maths adds up:** A member who freezes and returns, even for a year, is worth significantly more than the small amount you might recover by being inflexible. An inflexible approach can also lead to lost referrals and negative word-of-mouth.
*   **Structure is crucial:** While flexibility is good, it needs a clear, structured policy to prevent abuse, ensure consistency, and protect your revenue.
*   **Common freeze types:**
    *   **Medical freezes:** These are often necessary. Requesting documentation for longer periods (beyond two or three weeks) is reasonable.
    *   **Holiday pauses:** These can be capped, for example, at one or two per year, with defined minimum and maximum durations. A small, reduced fee during a holiday pause is a common and reasonable practice.
    *   **Paid freezes:** Members pay a reduced monthly amount to keep their membership active during uncertain times (e.g., job change, pregnancy, house move). The reduced rate should cover basic costs but not be so low that it encourages indefinite use. A common benchmark is the cost of locker rental.
    *   **Lower-tier options:** Offering a cheaper membership tier instead of cancellation can keep members paying something, especially if cost is their main reason for leaving.
*   **Monthly rolling contracts are expected:** Most members now expect this flexibility, so offering it is a baseline, not a unique selling point. This means your gym's quality and relationship management become even more important for retention.
*   **Inconsistency erodes trust:** The biggest problem with freeze policies is often a lack of consistency, leading to different staff giving different answers and members feeling unfairly treated.
*   **Clear policy details:** A good freeze policy should state available types, conditions, durations, fees, how to request one, and what happens when it ends. This information should be in the membership agreement and known by all staff.
*   **Limits are essential:** Set clear maximums for individual freezes and total freezes per year to ensure financial viability. These limits should be communicated upfront.
*   **Software is vital:** Relying on manual tracking for freezes will likely lead to errors. Membership management software should automatically handle billing pauses and restarts, and send automated confirmation and reminder messages to members.
*   **Prompt and clear communication:** Members appreciate quick, clear responses to freeze requests. Automated confirmations, check-ins for longer freezes, and reminders before a freeze ends are basic expectations.

## What a gym can do

*   **Develop a clear freeze policy:** Define specific types of freezes (medical, holiday, paid, lower-tier options), their conditions, maximum durations, and any associated fees.
*   **Communicate the policy:** Ensure the freeze policy is included in membership agreements, known by all staff, and readily available to members.
*   **Set reasonable limits:** Establish clear maximum durations for individual freezes and total freezes within a year to maintain financial stability.
*   **Utilise membership management software:** Implement software that can automatically pause and restart direct debits, track freeze dates, and send automated communications to members (confirmations, check-ins, return reminders).
*   **Train staff:** Ensure all staff members are fully aware of the freeze policy and can discuss it compassionately and professionally with members, especially regarding sensitive topics like injury or finances.
*   **Offer lower-tier alternatives:** Integrate the option of a reduced-price membership tier into your cancellation process, either through automated system prompts or as a standard question from staff.
*   **Provide monthly rolling contracts:** Offer this flexibility as it is now a common expectation for gym memberships.
*   **Prioritise consistent communication:** Ensure members receive prompt confirmation of freeze requests, check-in messages for longer pauses, and reminders before their membership reactivates.
*   **Review and refine:** Regularly assess how your freeze policy is working in practice, identifying areas for improvement and ensuring it aligns with your gym's values and desired perception in the local community.
