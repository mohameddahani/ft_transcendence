# The Guide to Reducing Churn at Your Gym (Glofox)

*A summary in our own words of [the original article](https://www.glofox.com/blog/reducing-churn/). Numbers in it appear in the original.*

## Key points

*   **Churn is costly:** Losing a member means losing their future revenue and the money spent to acquire them. It's also a personal blow for boutique studios that thrive on connection.
*   **Churn rate calculation:** To find your churn rate, divide the number of members who left by your total members at the start of a period, then multiply by 100. For example, 10 cancellations from 200 members gives a 5% monthly churn.
*   **Impact of churn:** A 5% monthly churn rate can lead to a 46% annual loss of members if not addressed. Reducing churn is almost always cheaper than finding new members, with a small increase in retention potentially boosting profits significantly.
*   **Reasons for leaving:** Members often leave due to a lack of visible progress, poor or absent onboarding, exercising alone, billing issues, a decline in perceived value, or a lack of timely interactions at predictable points (around 90 days, 6-7 months, and 12 months).
*   **Spotting at-risk members:** Disengagement, like reduced class attendance or last-minute cancellations, are key indicators. Members who complain are valuable as they provide feedback, unlike those who quietly leave.
*   **Industry benchmarks:** The average churn rate in the health and fitness industry in 2025 was 7.1%. Studios generally have higher retention rates (around 75.9%) compared to larger fitness facilities (mid-60% range).

## What a gym can do

*   **Implement structured onboarding:** The first 90 days are critical. A robust onboarding process, including goal-setting and regular check-ins, dramatically improves retention. Members who feel supported and connected early on are more likely to stay.
*   **Foster community:** Members with social connections to your studio stay longer. Encourage this through group classes, fitness challenges, recognition programs, and social events. This is especially important for smaller studios.
*   **Personalize communication:** Generic messages are ineffective. Tailor your communication based on member behavior, such as their membership duration and engagement patterns, to make messages more impactful.
*   **Address billing issues:** Prevent involuntary churn by fixing problems like expired cards and declined payments. Automated payment retries, credit card expiration reminders, and flexible payment options can help recover lost revenue.
*   **Utilize feedback loops:** Don't wait for exit surveys. Use NPS scores and regular instructor check-ins to gather feedback proactively. Even a quick question after class can uncover issues before they lead to cancellations.
*   **Show progress:** Help members see their achievements through milestone tracking, attendance streaks, and personalized messages. This reinforces the value of their membership and motivates them to continue.
*   **Leverage technology:** Studio management software can automate retention tasks like tracking attendance, sending personalized follow-ups, and identifying at-risk members. This allows staff to focus on member interaction and ensures consistency, especially for multiple locations.
