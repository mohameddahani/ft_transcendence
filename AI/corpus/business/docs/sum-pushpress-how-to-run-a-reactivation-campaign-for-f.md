# How to Run a Reactivation Campaign for Former Gym Members (PushPress)

*A summary in our own words of [the original article](https://www.pushpress.com/blog/how-to-run-a-reactivation-campaign-for-former-gym-members). Numbers in it appear in the original.*

## Key points

*   **Former members are valuable leads:** They already know your gym, have experienced your services, and are often easier and cheaper to win back than new customers. Many left due to life changes, not dissatisfaction.
*   **Consistent outreach is key:** A successful strategy involves contacting every former member three times a year, roughly every four months, with a personal, low-pressure check-in.
*   **The "permission to bug you" approach works:** Start conversations by acknowledging the time gap and asking for permission to check in occasionally. This makes future contact feel welcome.
*   **Multi-channel communication is effective:** Use a mix of phone calls (highest converting), text messages, emails, and even handwritten notes to reach different segments of your former members.
*   **Persistence pays off:** Don't give up after one or two attempts. Some members may take many touchpoints over a year or more to return.
*   **Systematize the process:** Block out dedicated time, assign someone ownership, track all outreach, and review results regularly to ensure the campaign happens consistently.
*   **Focus on a smooth return:** When a former member shows interest, make re-enrollment easy and welcoming. A free trial or a simplified sign-up can help, but discounts aren't always necessary as they already understand your value.
*   **Measure your success:** Track how many former members you contact, how many respond, and how many re-enroll. A return rate of five to ten percent annually can significantly boost revenue.

## What a gym can do

*   **Build a clean list of former members:** Gather data on everyone who cancelled or lapsed in the last two to three years. Segment them into tiers based on how recently they left (within six months, six to eighteen months, and over eighteen months).
*   **Adopt a three-times-a-year outreach cadence:** Plan specific times, such as January/February, May/June, and September/October, to reach out to your entire former member list.
*   **Use the "permission to bug you" script:** When making initial contact, use a phrase that acknowledges the time since they left and asks if it's okay to check in a couple of times a year.
*   **Utilize multiple communication channels:** For each touchpoint, use at least two methods like email for the full list, followed by personal phone calls to your most recent former members.
*   **Commit to the long game:** Understand that winning members back can take time and multiple attempts. Maintain a patient and caring approach.
*   **Systemize the reactivation campaign:** Schedule dedicated time for outreach, assign a team member to manage the process, track all interactions in your CRM or a spreadsheet, and create flexible templates for communication.
*   **Create a welcoming re-enrollment experience:** Remove any barriers for returning members. Offer a free trial, a quick goal check-in with a coach, and a simplified sign-up process.
*   **Track key metrics:** Monitor your outreach completion rate, response rate, and return rate to understand the effectiveness of your campaign and make improvements.
