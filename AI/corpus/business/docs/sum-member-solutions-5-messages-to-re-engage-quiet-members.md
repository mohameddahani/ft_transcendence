# 5 Messages to Re-Engage Quiet Members (Member Solutions)

*A summary in our own words of [the original article](https://membersolutions.com/blog/5-messages-that-keep-members-engaged-when-they-stop-showing-up/). Numbers in it appear in the original.*

## Key points

*   **Inactive members are in limbo:** Most members who stop attending haven't decided to quit; they've just lost momentum and are waiting to see if anyone notices their absence.
*   **Silence is detrimental:** When a member stops coming and receives no communication, they may feel unnoticed and unimportant, which often leads to cancellation.
*   **Timely and personal outreach is crucial:** Reaching out with a short, personal message within two weeks of reduced attendance can prevent members from drifting away.
*   **Focus on care, not sales:** Effective messages avoid guilt, sales language, or generic "we miss you" phrases. Instead, they express genuine concern and offer a clear, easy next step.
*   **Specific messages for different situations:** The article provides five distinct message types tailored to various stages of disengagement, from a two-week absence to a cancellation request or a long-term absence.
*   **Addressing common barriers:** Messages should aim to solve logistical problems (like schedule changes) or emotional barriers (like embarrassment after a long absence).
*   **Freeze requests are opportunities:** Many freeze requests are not final decisions but rather an opening for a conversation to find a middle ground, potentially retaining a significant percentage of members.
*   **Cancellation requests require empathy:** Slowing down the cancellation process with a caring inquiry can uncover fixable problems and show the member they are valued.
*   **Win-back strategies for long absences:** For members absent for a month or more, offering a "restart" session can overcome embarrassment and significantly increase return rates.
*   **Text is often best:** Text messages generally have the highest open rate and feel most personal for initial outreach.

## What a gym can do

*   **Monitor attendance:** Regularly identify members who have missed two consecutive weeks of their usual attendance pattern.
*   **Send a check-in message:** For members absent for two weeks, send a personal message asking if everything is okay and offering help with schedule adjustments, emphasizing that their spot is always there.
*   **Offer schedule alternatives:** If a member mentions being busy or having a changing schedule, proactively suggest specific alternative class times or formats to help them maintain their routine.
*   **Address freeze requests strategically:** When a member asks to freeze, offer alternatives like a reduced plan or different schedule before processing the freeze, aiming to keep them engaged.
*   **Respond to cancellation requests with inquiry:** Upon receiving a cancellation request, ask what prompted the decision to see if any adjustments can be made, showing care for the individual.
*   **Implement a "restart" program:** For members absent for a month or more, offer a private, no-pressure "restart" session with a coach to ease them back into the gym and overcome potential embarrassment.
*   **Use appropriate communication channels:** Prioritize text messages for initial personal outreach, using email for longer messages or links, and DMs if that's the member's preferred interaction method.
*   **Follow up judiciously:** Send up to two messages, spaced about seven to ten days apart. If there's no response, respect their silence but know the door remains open.
*   **Be prepared for various responses:** Listen to member feedback, acknowledge their concerns, and be honest about what can and cannot be done, demonstrating care even if a problem can't be fully solved.
*   **Act quickly:** Identify and reach out to at-risk members immediately, as timely intervention is key to re-engagement.
