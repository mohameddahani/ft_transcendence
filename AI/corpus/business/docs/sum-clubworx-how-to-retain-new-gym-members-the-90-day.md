# How to Retain New Gym Members: The 90-Day Retention Strategy (Clubworx)

*A summary in our own words of [the original article](https://www.clubworx.com/blog/how-to-retain-new-gym-members-the-90-day-retention-strategy-that-works). Numbers in it appear in the original.*

## Key points

*   A structured 90-day onboarding process significantly boosts new member retention, with gyms using one retaining 85% of new members compared to 68% for those without. This difference can mean a substantial amount of lost annual revenue for a gym.
*   The initial 90 days are crucial for new members to establish fitness as a regular habit. If the gym isn't integrated into their life by day 90, later incentives are unlikely to change that.
*   The first week (days one to seven) is about orientation and preventing confusion. New members need clear guidance on how to use the gym and what to do next.
*   The habit-forming period (days eight to 42) is when consistent attendance is vital. Members who visit at least twice a week during their first six weeks are much more likely to remain active long-term.
*   The decision point (days 43 to 90) is when members either become regulars or start to disengage. It's important to notice declining attendance and engage in progress-focused conversations.
*   Seasonal attendance dips can impact new members whose habits aren't yet solidified. Proactive check-ins before these periods are highly effective.
*   Effective retention relies on consistency, not just effort. Automating routine communications allows staff to focus on meaningful personal interactions.

## What a gym can do

*   Implement a structured 90-day onboarding program for all new members.
*   Send a welcome message via SMS or email within 24 hours of sign-up, clearly outlining what to expect for their first visit.
*   Follow up after their first session to ask how it went and provide specific recommendations for their next visit, rather than generic encouragement.
*   Track attendance, especially during weeks two to six, and reach out personally if a new member's expected attendance pattern drops.
*   Acknowledge milestones, such as a member's tenth visit or first month, to make them feel valued.
*   Proactively suggest different classes or time slots during weeks two to six to help members explore options and build a broader connection to the gym.
*   Conduct a progress conversation around the 60-day mark, focusing on their goals and experience, rather than a sales or retention pitch.
*   Automate consistent communications like welcome messages, session reminders, milestone notifications, and alerts for missed sessions.
*   Reserve staff time for personal interactions, such as the first-week check-in call and the 60-day progress conversation, as these are the moments members remember.
