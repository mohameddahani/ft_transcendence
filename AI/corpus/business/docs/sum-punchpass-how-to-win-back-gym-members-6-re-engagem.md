# How to Win Back Gym Members: 6 Re-Engagement Strategies (Punchpass)

*A summary in our own words of [the original article](https://punchpass.com/resources/blog/how-to-win-back-gym-members-6-re-engagement-strategies/). Numbers in it appear in the original.*

## Key points

*   It's more effective and profitable to focus on keeping and re-engaging existing members than constantly trying to find new ones.
*   The average health club loses about a quarter of its members each year, and personal trainers see an even higher turnover.
*   Attracting new customers costs five times more than keeping current ones.
*   You have a much higher chance of selling to someone who is already a customer (between 60% and 70%) compared to a new prospect.
*   Even a small increase in member retention (five percent) can significantly boost your profits, anywhere from 25% to 95%.
*   It's crucial to regularly track your overall annual retention rate, how often members attend classes, and which pricing plans lead to the most frequent attendance.
*   Instead of generic "we miss you" messages, a more effective approach is to analyze your gym's operations, value, and culture over the past six to twelve months to understand why members might be leaving or attending less.
*   After the pandemic, many people are hesitant about long-term commitments or indoor workouts, and you're now competing with online options and home gyms.

## What a gym can do

*   Regularly review your attendance and membership data to understand your retention rate and which pricing packages are most popular.
*   Ask yourself key questions about your operations, such as whether you offer classes at peak times, if you have high instructor turnover, how often you communicate with members, and how flexible you are with their needs.
*   If you're unsure about these questions, survey your members for specific feedback on your services and satisfaction levels.
*   Clearly show the value you provide by comparing yourself to competitors, offering excellent customer service, and designing classes and schedules based on member feedback.
*   Maintain consistent communication through multiple channels, sending weekly emails about updates, promotions, and health tips, and posting regularly on social media.
*   Be responsive to inquiries, comments, and reviews on your inbox, social media, and Google My Business listing.
*   Build meaningful relationships with members through personal emails, social media interactions, and in-studio conversations.
*   Foster a sense of community by hosting social events, either after classes or outside the gym, like casual gatherings or demonstrations.
*   Increase flexibility by considering shorter contracts, removing advance cancellation notice requirements, not charging for membership pauses, offering incentives for returning long-term customers, and providing combined in-person and online class options.
*   Re-introduce your team, instructors, and class schedule to current and past members, highlighting new programs and people.
*   Use the insights from member surveys to upgrade your programming, potentially adjusting class lengths, intensity, or types, and eliminating unpopular options.
*   Communicate any new improvements through your website, email, and social media to generate excitement and encourage members to return.
