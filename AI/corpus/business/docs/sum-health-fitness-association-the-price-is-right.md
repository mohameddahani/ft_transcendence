# The Price is Right (Health & Fitness Association)

*A summary in our own words of [the original article](https://www.healthandfitness.org/the-price-is-right/). Numbers in it appear in the original.*

## Key points

*   **Inflation and Rising Costs:** Gyms are facing increased costs for goods and labor, leading many to consider or implement price increases. Even large, affordable chains like Planet Fitness have raised their basic membership price for the first time in many years.
*   **Pandemic Impact:** The pandemic forced many fitness centers to raise prices to survive, with a significant percentage of clubs increasing dues by a few dollars per month. It also led to a mass evaluation of pricing across the industry.
*   **Growth in Fitness Interest:** A positive outcome of the pandemic was a heightened focus on health and fitness, attracting new members to gyms who are more dedicated and stay longer.
*   **Thriving Models:** Both very low-price (HVLP) models and premier clubs (with membership rates of eighty dollars or more per month) are currently doing well. Some premier clubs are even reporting their best revenue in decades.
*   **Value is Key:** Regardless of price point, a strong value proposition is crucial. This means offering unique value beyond just a commodity, understanding what different customer categories perceive as valuable, and focusing on an exceptional customer experience.
*   **Strategic Price Adjustments:** Price increases should be thoughtful and purposeful, connecting to your brand and brand promise. Some facilities opt for planned annual or biannual increases of a small percentage to reduce surprise.
*   **Transparency in Communication:** When raising prices, it's essential to communicate clearly and transparently with members about why the increase is happening (e.g., to pay staff more, invest in equipment, cover rising operational costs).
*   **Cancellations Not Guaranteed:** While some attrition may occur, price increases don't automatically lead to a spike in cancellations, especially if the gym provides outstanding experiences and perceived value. Other factors like overall club experience or competitor offerings often play a larger role.
*   **Industry Optimism:** The fitness industry is currently booming, with memberships and revenue on the rise. Consumers are also less price-sensitive than in the past and more willing to pay for something they value.

## What a gym can do

*   **Evaluate Your Pricing Strategy:** Review your current pricing in light of rising operational costs and market trends. Consider if your prices accurately reflect your demographics, operating expenses, and the value you provide.
*   **Strengthen Your Value Proposition:** Clearly define what makes your gym unique and valuable. For non-HVLP models, focus on wrapping your product in a service and a proven process to differentiate yourself from competitors.
*   **Prioritize Member Experience:** Be intentional about every aspect of the member experience, from greetings and staff dress code to cleanliness and how service errors are handled. An exceptional experience can justify higher prices and encourage loyalty and referrals.
*   **Understand Customer Perceived Value:** Recognize that different customer segments will value different aspects of your gym. Tailor your offerings and communication to resonate with these varied perceptions of value.
*   **Plan Price Adjustments Strategically:** If you decide to raise prices, do so thoughtfully. Consider a small, regular increase (e.g., three to five percent every one to two years) rather than large, infrequent jumps.
*   **Communicate Transparently:** When implementing a price change, inform members well in advance through multiple channels (email, SMS, social media, letters). Explain the reasons behind the increase, such as investments in staff, equipment, or facilities, to foster understanding.
*   **Equip Your Team:** Train your staff thoroughly on the reasons for any price changes and how to address member questions positively and productively. Provide them with FAQs and role-playing opportunities.
*   **Continuously Invest:** Keep investing in your products and services. Members are more likely to accept price increases when they see ongoing improvements and value being added to their membership.
*   **Focus on Community:** Build a strong, winning community within your gym. This can help create true value and make your facility a desirable fitness destination, protecting against cancellations even with price adjustments.
