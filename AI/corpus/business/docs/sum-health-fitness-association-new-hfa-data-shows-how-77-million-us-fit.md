# New HFA Data Shows How 77 Million US Fitness Facility Members Work Out (Health & Fitness Association)

*A summary in our own words of [the original article](https://www.healthandfitness.org/new-hfa-data-shows-how-77-million-us-fitness-facility-members-work-out/). Numbers in it appear in the original.*

## Key points

*   A new report from the Health & Fitness Association (HFA) provides detailed insights into how 77 million US fitness facility members used their gyms in 2024.
*   Traditional equipment like treadmills and free weights remain central to the in-club experience.
*   There's a growing trend towards activities focused on balance and long-term health, with yoga participation increasing and Pilates exceeding eight percent.
*   Racquet sports, particularly pickleball, are seeing a significant resurgence, with pickleball participation more than doubling from three percent in 2021 to eight percent in 2024 among gym members.
*   Professional coaching is still important, with nearly a quarter of members using a personal trainer and one-third participating in small-group training.
*   However, members are using personal training less frequently, averaging 21 sessions per year, down from 28 in 2019.
*   Women were a key driver of growth in personal training, increasing by sixteen percent year-over-year to 7.3 million users.
*   Teens and Gen X adults showed the most growth in small-group training.
*   A significant number of members, over a quarter, hold memberships at more than one facility, and over three-quarters of studio users also have at least one additional membership.
*   The report highlights that people are combining different types of equipment, training formats, and coaching to create varied fitness routines, with a focus on longevity, recovery, and balance.

## What a gym can do

*   Recognize that while traditional equipment like treadmills and free weights are still popular, there's a clear shift towards activities that promote balance and longevity. Consider expanding or highlighting offerings in yoga and Pilates.
*   Capitalize on the growing interest in racquet sports, especially pickleball, by exploring options to incorporate these activities or partner with local facilities.
*   Understand that professional coaching remains valuable, but members are using personal training less often. This might suggest a need to re-evaluate personal training packages or offer more flexible options.
*   Tailor marketing and programming to specific demographics, noting that women are driving growth in personal training and teens and Gen X adults are leading gains in small-group training.
*   Acknowledge the trend of multi-club memberships. This could mean focusing on unique offerings that complement other facilities or considering partnerships to provide a broader fitness ecosystem for members.
*   Emphasize the benefits of fitness as a lifelong pursuit, focusing on recovery, balance, and overall well-term health in your messaging and program design.
