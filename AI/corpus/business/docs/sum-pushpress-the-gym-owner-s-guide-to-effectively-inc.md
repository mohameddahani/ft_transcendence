# The Gym Owner's Guide to Effectively Increasing Gym Membership Prices (PushPress)

*A summary in our own words of [the original article](https://www.pushpress.com/blog/increasing-gym-membership-prices). Numbers in it appear in the original.*

## Key points

*   Many gym owners undercharge for their services, often due to fear of member reactions to price increases.
*   Increasing prices is crucial for your gym's profitability and financial viability.
*   Your services, including your team's time, knowledge, and experience, are valuable and should be priced appropriately.
*   A unique selling proposition (USP) helps justify price increases by highlighting what makes your gym special and the value it provides.
*   It's important to calculate your new pricing based on your gym's needs, not on what competitors charge.
*   Simplifying membership options, ideally to no more than three levels, can make management easier and improve sales.
*   Consider eliminating gym membership discounts to attract clients who value your services.
*   Provide a grace period between announcing a price increase and its implementation, such as an upcoming billing cycle.
*   While some member churn or negative feedback is possible, it's usually not as widespread as feared.
*   Be prepared to discuss concerns with members and understand that some may not be your target audience.

## What a gym can do

*   **Define and highlight your USP:** Clearly articulate what makes your gym unique and why members should choose you over competitors. Use this to justify any price increases by showing how you continue to add value, such as through facility improvements, new equipment, additional classes, or open gym hours.
*   **Calculate new prices accurately:** Do not guess or base your pricing on competitors. Use a structured method to determine the exact rates you need to charge to ensure profitability. The article mentions a downloadable "Price Increase Guide" and calculator to help with this.
*   **Simplify membership structures:** Review and restructure your current membership options, aiming to reduce them to a maximum of three levels. This simplifies management and makes it easier for prospective members to understand and choose a plan.
*   **Eliminate discounts:** Consider phasing out gym membership discounts. The focus should be on providing value and solutions to members' problems, which will justify the price you ask.
*   **Communicate clearly and with a grace period:** When announcing price increases, use a clear communication method, such as an email template provided in the guide. Ensure there's a reasonable grace period, like an upcoming billing cycle, between the announcement and when the new prices take effect. This allows members time to process the information and ask questions.
*   **Prepare for feedback and potential churn:** Anticipate that some members might express concerns or even cancel their memberships. Be ready to have conversations with those who have questions and understand that losing a few members is normal and often not as significant as initially feared. The article suggests anticipating the number of members you can afford to lose.
