# Why Onboarding and Retention Beats Marketing Every Time (Glofox)

*A summary in our own words of [the original article](https://www.glofox.com/blog/onboarding-and-retention/). Numbers in it appear in the original.*

## Key points

*   **Onboarding is crucial for retention:** Most members leave within the first ninety days, not due to price, but because they don't know how to succeed. Effective onboarding is a sustained process, not just a quick tour, and significantly increases the likelihood of members staying active after six months.
*   **Focus on the first ninety days:** This period is the most impactful for retention. Gyms that keep members long-term have structured processes for these initial weeks, including planned communication and clear next steps.
*   **Three pillars of effective onboarding:**
    *   **Expectation-setting:** Clearly communicate what members can expect from the gym and what the gym needs from them.
    *   **Confidence-building:** Help new members feel comfortable and competent by providing equipment orientations, trainer introductions, and guidance on beginner classes.
    *   **Habit formation:** Support members in making the gym a regular part of their routine, extending beyond the first visit until the habit is established.
*   **Retention is more profitable than acquisition:** Acquiring a new member can cost up to five times more than keeping an existing one. Improving retention rates by just five percent can boost profits by as much as ninety-five percent.
*   **Connection matters more than cost:** Members who feel part of a community are more likely to stay. Research indicates that social interaction is a primary reason active consumers stick with fitness.
*   **Discounts don't solve retention issues:** Price cuts address the wrong problem. Members leave because they lack habits, confidence, or connection, not because of the cost. Redirecting marketing budgets to structured onboarding yields better returns.

## What a gym can do

*   **Implement a structured ninety-day onboarding program:** Design a sustained process that goes beyond a simple orientation, focusing on setting expectations, building confidence, and fostering habit formation.
*   **Map out consistent touch points:** Plan specific communications and interactions for new members during their first week and month. This includes welcome messages, check-ins after first workouts, invitations to classes, and introductions to community features.
*   **Provide clear next steps:** After every interaction, ensure members know exactly what to do next, whether it's booking a class, trying a new activity, or following a thirty-day plan.
*   **Maintain consistent communication and accountability:** Use regular touch points like automated class reminders, milestone celebrations, and re-engagement messages for members whose visit frequency drops.
*   **Personalize experiences using data:** Leverage member data, such as goals and visit patterns, to tailor communications automatically. This allows for personalized attention without increasing staff workload.
*   **Utilize software for automation:** Employ gym management software to automate workflows, such as welcome sequences, follow-up check-ins after first visits, and nudges for missed weeks. This ensures consistent engagement and prevents members from being overlooked.
*   **Track visits and identify at-risk members:** Use software to monitor attendance patterns and proactively identify members who haven't visited in a while, allowing your team to reach out before they consider canceling.
*   **Integrate communication channels:** Use a single platform for all member communications (SMS, email) to ensure consistent messaging.
*   **Standardize processes across locations:** For gyms with multiple sites, use a centralized system to maintain consistent onboarding experiences and track retention metrics across all locations.
