# Why Gym Payments Fail: Common Causes & How to Prevent (ABC Fitness)

*A summary in our own words of [the original article](https://abcfitness.com/abc-articles/failed-gym-payments/). Numbers in it appear in the original.*

## Key points

*   **Failed payments are different from debt:** A payment failure is a recent, recoverable issue, while debt collection is for accounts that have been past-due for a long time. Treating them the same way can cost you money you could have recovered.
*   **Common reasons for payment failure:** The main causes are expired or outdated cards, insufficient funds (often around paydays), and bank or processor issues like fraud flags. These usually don't mean a member wants to cancel.
*   **Neobanks and app-based accounts:** A growing number of members use app-based banking (like Cash App or Venmo), which can lead to higher rates of insufficient funds due to lower balances, members blocking recurring charges directly from the app, and income volatility from gig work.
*   **Cost of failed payments:** Beyond lost revenue, each unresolved failed payment takes up staff time for manual follow-up, pulling them away from other duties. The longer a payment remains unresolved, the harder it is to recover.
*   **Industry estimates:** Between seven and twelve percent of monthly membership transactions fail due to issues like expired cards, insufficient funds, blocked accounts, and risk-related declines.

## What a gym can do

*   **Prevent failures proactively:**
    *   **Keep card details current:** Use card updater tools that automatically refresh expiring or reissued cards through card networks.
    *   **Capture backup payment methods:** At signup, collect a secondary payment method so the system can try it if the primary one fails, reducing the need for staff intervention.
    *   **Use instant bank linking for ACH:** This removes manual entry of bank details, which often causes errors, by allowing members to authenticate their bank account directly.
*   **Time billing strategically:**
    *   **Send pre-billing notifications:** Notify members two or three days before a charge is due, giving them time to update cards or move funds, preventing "surprise declines."
    *   **Offer flexible billing cadences:** Instead of billing everyone on the first of the month, consider biweekly billing or allowing members to select their preferred payment date to align with varied pay cycles, especially for those with irregular income.
*   **Respond quickly and efficiently to failures:**
    *   **Fast, low-friction first outreach:** Send a friendly notification within twenty-four hours of a failed payment, making it easy for the member to resolve the issue quickly, such as with a one-step payment link on their mobile device.
    *   **Intelligent retry logic:** Instead of a fixed retry schedule (e.g., every three days), use a system that considers the reason for the decline. For example, retry an insufficient funds decline after a likely payday, or prompt for a card update for an expired card. This increases the chances of successful recovery.
