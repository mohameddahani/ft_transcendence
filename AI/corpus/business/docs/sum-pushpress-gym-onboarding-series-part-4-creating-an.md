# Gym Onboarding Series, Part 4: Creating An Unforgettable First 90 Days (PushPress)

*A summary in our own words of [the original article](https://www.pushpress.com/blog/gym-onboarding-series-part-4-creating-an-unforgettable-first-90-days). Numbers in it appear in the original.*

## Key points

*   The first 90 days are crucial for new member retention, as most members who quit during this period do so because they haven't formed a habit of attending, not because they dislike the program.
*   New members need to displace existing routines with gym attendance, which takes time, potentially up to 90 days for a habit like gym attendance that involves discomfort.
*   Gyms with a structured 90-day system can significantly reduce their monthly quit rate, with one expert achieving a 3% rate compared to an industry average of 5–7%.
*   The system involves three non-negotiable steps: building the habit of showing up, helping members develop a love for training, and painting a long-term picture of their future at the gym.
*   Personalized communication, especially after a missed class, is highly effective for retention, but it must be human and caring, not automated or generic.
*   Encouraging members to verbally commit to their next class increases their likelihood of showing up due to social accountability.
*   Helping members articulate what they love about the gym, ideally within the first two weeks, helps them "sell themselves" on the program and provides a reason to return.
*   Coaches should actively identify and acknowledge "peak experiences" or "aha moments" for new members, turning fleeting sensations into memorable achievements.
*   By day 60, members should have a clear vision of their future at the gym, which can be painted by highlighting past successes of long-term members and using the new member's own progress data.
*   A 10-minute, in-person or phone check-in at day 90 is essential to discuss unexpected positives, future goals, and any potential friction points that could lead to cancellation.

## What a gym can do

*   **Build the habit of showing up:**
    *   At sign-up, proactively schedule new members into specific classes rather than asking what sounds good, treating these times as non-optional.
    *   Send a personalized text message from a coach within 10 minutes of a missed scheduled class, offering to help them reschedule. Avoid automated, generic texts in the first 30 days.
    *   After every class, ask new members when they are coming back and get a specific commitment (e.g., "Wednesday at 6").
*   **Help them develop a love for training:**
    *   Assign each new member to a coach for their first 30 days.
    *   Within the first two weeks, have the assigned coach ask the new member, one-on-one, "What do you love most about training here?" and log their answer.
    *   Coaches should actively watch for and verbally acknowledge "aha moments" or peak experiences during training, turning them into memorable achievements.
*   **Paint the long-term picture:**
    *   On the gym floor, point out long-tenure members to new members, highlighting their progress and showing a concrete example of what's possible.
    *   By week three or four, use the new member's own progress data (e.g., first class results, body composition changes) to show their improvements and motivate them.
    *   At day 90, schedule a 10-minute in-person or phone check-in to ask about unexpected loves, future goals, and what would need to change for them to stay for the next five years.
*   **Implement a comprehensive 90-day checklist:**
    *   Set standing appointments for the first 30 days.
    *   Enroll members in a "Committed Club" to track attendance against a goal (e.g., 12 classes in 30 days).
    *   Log a measurable baseline for each new member.
    *   Introduce new members to at least three long-tenure members by name.
    *   Publicly celebrate one win for the new member (e.g., class shout-out, gym group chat).
    *   Reset goals and commitments for the next 90 days during the day 90 check-in.
